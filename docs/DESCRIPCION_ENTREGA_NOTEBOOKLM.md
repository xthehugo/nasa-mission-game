## Detailed Engineering & Mission Architecture Narrative

**ORBITAL FORGE** translates institutional spaceflight standards directly into deterministic simulation mechanics by converting the **NASA Systems Engineering Handbook** resource definitions into a live accounting engine where **Current Best Estimates (CBE)** and **Mass Growth Allowances (MGA)** dictate whether a design passes formal gateway milestones. Rather than treating technical margins as abstract text, our platform programmatically enforces **GSFC-STD-1000 ("GOLD Rules")** by auditing subsystem architectures against mandatory threshold gates—such as the requirement to identify and eliminate **Single Point Failures (Rule 1.05)**, maintaining a $\ge 25\%$ to $35\%$ technical margin pool for mass and electrical power at the **Mission Concept Review (MCR / CML 5)**, and verifying RF link viability. Ground communication budgets are governed by actual **DSN Telecommunications Link Design Handbook (DSN 810-005)** and **Near Space Network (NSN)** metrics, dynamically calculating space loss, frequency-dependent atmospheric attenuation, and receiver figure-of-merit ($G/T$) to verify that downlinks achieve the required $\ge 3\text{ dB}$ margin for deep-space and near-Earth passes.

To ensure astrodynamic rigor beyond closed-form approximations, the engine couples propulsion selection directly to trajectory models validated by the **NASA General Mission Analysis Tool (GMAT)**. While basic sizing utilizes **Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation**, real-world trajectory feasibility depends on the distinct operational regimes of high-thrust chemical systems versus continuous, low-thrust electric propulsion modules. By integrating mission profiles derived from GMAT's enterprise flight mechanics—incorporating higher-order planetary spherical harmonics, solar radiation pressure perturbations, and multi-body gravitational mechanics—**ORBITAL FORGE** verifies that a student's chosen propulsion subsystem and propellant mass budget provide the exact **Delta-V ($\Delta V$)** profile and thrust margins required to achieve orbit insertion and halo station-keeping across **LEO**, **Lunar**, **Lagrange $L_2$**, and **Deep Space** regimes.

This interactive architecture directly resolves the recognized educational dilemma in space science, where academic learning is historically compartmentalized into isolated topics such as orbital mechanics or CAD modeling without demonstrating how decisions interact across a complete mission system. By organizing progression through **JPL's Concept Maturity Levels (CML 1 to CML 5)** and simulating the concurrent engineering workflows of **JPL Team X** and **NASA Ames MDC**, students experience the exact trade-offs faced by actual flight programs. Whether balancing the extreme pointing requirements of Canada's **JWST Fine Guidance Sensor**, the formation-flying plasma bursts of **TRACERS**, or the strict rideshare mass envelopes of **Lunar Trailblazer**, participants stop performing disconnected homework calculations and instead master the multi-segment engineering synthesis demanded of future NASA and CSA mission architects.
